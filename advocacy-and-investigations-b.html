Advocacy & Investigations

Orthodox survivors face painful isolation and steep barriers to coming forward or achieving closure. Magen provides options and support, and you decide the path forward.

Livui & Chakirot in Action

Justice delayed is not justice denied, not if someone stands with you until the very end.

International Liaison

Three years ago, Levi called from America. He needed to tell someone about the teacher who had saved him, and destroyed him.

He was nine years old when it began. His home life was chaos and violence, and his teacher saw it. The teacher offered what looked like salvation: a safe space, a refuge, someone who cared. For a child desperate to escape, it felt like rescue.

But it was a trap. For years, the teacher exploited him, using the very vulnerability he'd promised to protect as a weapon. The boy grew into a man carrying that betrayal across an ocean, across decades.

When he finally reached out, we guided him through the legal labyrinth of filing charges from thousands of miles away. We helped him find the courage to fly to Israel and face a system that would demand he relive his trauma in official statements and interrogations.

The process was grueling. Legal deliberations stretched on. Then came the confrontation, sitting across from the man who had groomed him, forcing the truth into the open. And finally, a confession. The prosecutor's office filed an indictment.

A nine-year-old boy was betrayed by the person who promised to protect him. A grown man refused to let that betrayal be the end of the story.

Common Questions

FAQ

No, reporting to authorities is entirely your choice. We support survivors at their own pace and respect your decisions. We can provide information, emotional support, and help you understand your options without any pressure to formally report. Many survivors work with us for a while before deciding if and when to make a report. Our relationship with you does not depend on your decision to report.

Legal processes vary widely, often taking many months or even years to complete. Investigations can be lengthy, and court proceedings even longer. We believe in being transparent about these timeframes while providing continuous support throughout the entire journey. Our advocates will be with you for as long as you need, regardless of how long the process takes.

We strive to provide consistent support through the same advocate for your case. This consistency ensures your advocate fully understands your specific situation and needs. However, staff members also pursue further degrees and employment in this space, so additional staff may assist in specific circumstances where specialized expertise is needed. We have a weekly staff meeting catching up on all open cases; if anyone has to step in, a briefing and a meeting with you is all that's needed.

We start by developing a safety plan for the day, including transportation arrangements to and from court, and can cover taxi services when public transportation might compromise safety or privacy. We prepare you emotionally for the possibility of seeing the perpetrator, practicing grounding techniques and coping strategies beforehand. Our advocates coordinate with court officials to arrange separate waiting areas whenever possible, and can request staggered arrival and departure times to avoid encounters outside the courtroom. Inside the courtroom, we can request protective measures such as screens or video testimony in some cases, and our advocate physically positions themselves between you and the perpetrator during proceedings.

In Israel, special protections exist for child victims. Children under 14 typically do not testify in open court. Instead, they are interviewed by specially trained Child Investigators (חוקרי ילדים) in child-friendly environments, and these recorded interviews can often be used as evidence, eliminating the need for children to appear in court or face their abuser. For older minors who may need to testify, we coordinate with prosecutors and request accommodations such as testimony via closed-circuit television or privacy screens. A child's advocate prepares them for any court involvement in age-appropriate ways, and Magen works closely with families to ensure children receive psychological support before, during, and after proceedings.

We understand this challenge deeply. Many survivors navigate this journey without familial or communal support, some even face active shunning and silencing. Our advocates can help you develop strategies, be your support system and connect you with more, and in some cases work with community leaders to foster understanding.

Many survivors come forward years or even decades after the incidents. While there are legal considerations regarding statutes of limitations, our support services remain available regardless. We understand the complex reasons why disclosure often happens long after the events, and approach historical cases with the same dedication and care.

Police frequently request our assistance, as they know that trauma-informed support and insider knowledge can help secure a conviction in sensitive cases. Our advocates have established relationships with law enforcement and understand how to bridge the cultural gaps that often exist between religious communities and legal authorities. We maintain contact with investigators to keep you informed about your case status and advocate for your needs throughout the process.

Yes. Our multilingual, multi-disciplinary approach allows us to liaise between victims, local police, and international law enforcement bodies. We've helped secure the extradition of sexual abuser Malka Leifer to Australia, and located American abusers who had escaped to Israel. We remain deeply involved in ongoing international cases and have prevented known American and European abusers from obtaining Israeli citizenship.

Besides for Bituach Leumi applications, all support is provided free of charge.

If you're unsure where to start, you can reach out by phone, WhatsApp, or email. You don't need to have everything figured out, you can just begin.
