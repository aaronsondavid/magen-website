About Magen

Magen. #LoTishtok
Join the movement.

Our past, present, and future are wrapped up in this movement. Magen exists so that no survivor of sexual abuse has to navigate justice, healing, or their own community alone.

Talk to us Support our work

Magen holds two commitments at once: survivors, and the communities they come from.

Magen for survivors

Healing their past. Supporting their present. Preparing their future.

Every survivor we work with sets the pace, the depth, and the direction. Our job is to make sure the resources they need, from crisis support to therapy to legal accompaniment, are already in the room by the time they ask.

Magen for communities

Accountability for our past. Responsibility for our present. Policy for our future.

We work with rabbis, educators, journalists, and lawmakers to change the conditions that let abuse hide. Prevention, education, and honest reckoning are how communities become safer, and stay that way.

Achievements and impact

The proof is in the survivors supported, the perpetrators held accountable, and the communities that have changed.

Survivor advocacy

Magen has directly supported hundreds of survivors through complex legal, emotional, and communal challenges, at their own pace.

High-impact cases

From Malka Leifer to Chaim Walder to Yehuda Meshi-Zahav, Magen has played a pivotal role in tracking and holding international perpetrators accountable, so no abuser can outrun justice by crossing a border.

Cultural change

Through education, journalism partnerships, and steady advocacy, Magen has shifted communal norms to prioritize survivor safety, empowerment, and healing.

Our approachVictim-centered, from the first call to the last.

Abuse strips survivors of agency. Restoring that agency is the core value that shapes every decision Magen makes.

  • The right to decide their own path forward, at their own pace.
  • The right to prioritize themselves and their needs above anyone else's expectations.
  • The right to make mistakes and change course, as part of reclaiming autonomy.

How we got here

Fifteen years of building, learning, and being present for what our communities needed next.

2010
Established to address child abuse in Haredi and Dati communities in Beit Shemesh.
2012
Launched our first community workshops.
2016
Partnered with JCW, a US-based organization addressing sexual abuse in the Jewish community.
2017
Expanded our mandate nationally and internationally, focused services on sexual abuse.
2020
Became a fully independent Israeli organization.
2021
Entered Mercaz Siyua partnerships. High-profile case exposure including Chaim Walder and Meshi Zahav. Malka Leifer extradited to Australia.
2022
Held a women's awareness event that filled a 420-seat theater and led to 80 disclosures in the days after.
2023
Pivoted to help October 7 evacuees protect their children in hotel-shelter conditions.
2024
Logged 9,061 crisis contacts, supported 2,007 victims, and 197 active law-enforcement cases.
2025
Scaling the model. Deepening the reach.

Frequently asked questions

Straight answers to the questions we hear most, from survivors, families, donors, and community leaders.

What is Magen and what does it do?

Established in 2010, Magen addresses sexual abuse in Haredi and Dati communities by raising awareness and supporting survivors within these communities, as well as those who have left. We work across three core areas: crisis and mental-health support, advocacy, and education. Our aim is to break the silence that protects perpetrators, and to build a culture of support and safety in its place.

Which communities does Magen serve?

Magen primarily serves Haredi and Dati communities, which have historically been under-served by services that understand their unique worldviews. We also support individuals who have left these communities but still need culturally-informed assistance. Our office is in Beit Shemesh, but we work with clients across Israel, and internationally when there's an Israel connection to the case.

What are your privacy policies?

All communications with Magen are completely confidential. We only share information with your explicit consent, except in cases where there is immediate danger to you or others, such as mandated reporting situations. Every staff member signs a confidentiality policy.

We do not share your photos with donors. If you appear in an image we use, we blur you out. If we share a story, your details are anonymized.

How is Magen funded?

Magen is funded through a combination of private donations, family foundations, government grants, and crowdfunding. We're publicly accountable through our board and formal financial oversight. We also have fiscal sponsorship through P.E.F. Israel Endowment Funds, Inc., a registered US 501(c)(3).

Does Magen charge for services?

No. Magen's case-management services are provided free of charge to survivors and their families. Some group programs (like trauma yoga and empowered-mother courses) may involve a modest fee to cover facilitator costs, and subsidies are available if that's a barrier.

Is Magen affiliated with any religious or political organization?

Magen is an independent organization, not formally affiliated with any specific religious or political entity. We operate with deep respect for Torah values and halacha, and work collaboratively with community leaders across the religious spectrum.

How can I volunteer or work with Magen?

Magen welcomes community involvement through volunteer roles, from accompaniment volunteers who support survivors through medical or legal processes, to fundraising and event help. For those with professional experience in relevant fields (law, mental health, communications, education), we occasionally have paid opportunities. Reach out to discuss how you can contribute.

Is Magen's work halachically approved?

Yes. Magen firmly asserts that our work reflects the true values of Torah and halacha, which prioritize justice, protecting the vulnerable, and creating accountability. We don't defend our alignment with Torah so much as lead with confidence that this is what Torah and halacha demand in cases of abuse and community responsibility.

How does Magen work with rabbinical authorities?

We maintain constructive relationships with community institutions, grounded in mutual respect and a shared value for Torah and halacha. Our advocates meet with rabbis, community leaders, and municipal workers to ensure that families who have been ostracized can still access schools, synagogues, communities, and the benefits they're entitled to. We work strategically with community leaders to promote shared responsibility for making communities safer.
