The reality

By the age of 18, 1 in 5 children will experience sexual abuse. In nearly 90% of cases, the abuser is someone they know.

And the numbers are no different, even in our most insular communities. For years, perpetrators groomed and evaded justice. But that is changing: survivors are finding their voices, and a safer future is within reach for all of us.

When systems fail, someone must refuse to look away

One survivor's courage. Years of advocacy. Finally, accountability.

When a young man reached out to Magen, he carried a burden he'd held since he was 15: abuse by his Rosh Yeshiva.

He had watched other victims press criminal charges, only to see their abuser walk away with a lenient plea deal. The message was clear, the system had failed them. But he refused to stay silent.

He came to us seeking a different path: a civil suit. We connected him with legal representation and began building his case. Then we made a disturbing discovery, despite a prior conviction, his abuser had returned to running the very same yeshiva, with access to countless vulnerable students.

We reported him to the police. We reported him to the Ministry of Education. Again and again. Nothing happened. So we made a different choice: we brought the case to the media. Within weeks, police finally took action, questioning the abuser for violating the laws that bar convicted sex offenders from working with children.

One survivor's courage. Years of persistent advocacy. And finally, accountability.

Our journey

2010
Magen founded in Beit Shemesh to address abuse in Haredi and Dati communities.
2015–16
Expanded nationally and internationally; partnered with Lo Tishtok to erase the stigma around abuse.
2020
Became an independent organization.
2021
A watershed moment: Malka Leifer extradited after a years-long fight for accountability.
2022
A 420-person women's awareness event, 80 disclosures followed within days.
Today
Expanding digital resources, international partnerships, and systemic advocacy.
