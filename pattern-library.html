If Your Case Was Closed

A closed case does not mean you weren't believed.

Understanding the process 7 minute read Updated July 2026

In 2022, 71% of sexual-offense cases in Israel closed without an indictment. If your case was one of them, the pain of that letter is real — and you are far from alone in receiving it.

The most common reason a case closes is lack of evidenceGlossaryThe most common reason for a case to close (~65% of suspects). It means the evidence gathered was not strong enough to meet the very high standard required for a criminal conviction — not that you were disbelieved.Read the full definition →. This is about what a court needs to convict, not about whether the prosecutor believed you happened. The legal standard — beyond a reasonable doubtGlossaryThe highest standard of proof in Israeli law, required for a criminal conviction. Civil cases use a much lower standard (balance of probabilities).Read the full definition → — is deliberately very high, and most sex-crime evidence does not clear it.

You know what happened. A closed case does not diminish that truth, or the courage it took to come forward.

Your rights right now

You have the right to know the exact reason the case was closed. If the letter doesn't say, you can request that information from any police station or through the MENA systemGlossaryThe Crime Victim Information System — a portal where you can track your case status. You receive a code and password when you file a complaint.Read the full definition →.

You also have the right to appeal the closure within 60 days of receiving the notice. Appeals succeed rarely — but they do succeed, especially when new evidence emerges. Legal aid can help you write one.

Feeling the closure right now? Talk to someone.

MENA System

מערכת מידע לנפגעי עבירה (מנ"ע)

The MENA System (מערכת מנ"ע — Ma'arechet Meida Le-Nifgei Aveira, "Crime Victim Information System") is a computerized portal where you can track the progress of your case from the moment you file a complaint through to the sentencing of the accused.

You receive an access code and personal password when you file the complaint at the police station. You can also request them by phone at 02-5017030.
